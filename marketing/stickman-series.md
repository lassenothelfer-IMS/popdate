# Longer stickman stories for Popdate (scripts, 2026-11-05)

Follow-up to `stickman-reels.md` and `stickman/` (the first Reel, "The quiet one"). This file covers how to make the stories longer without losing viewers: one 5-part series with recurring characters, and 5 standalone stories of 45 to 75 seconds.

Same rules as before: no show footage, never "official" or "the Pop the Balloon app", the goal is waitlist signups.

## What the research says about longer stories

- **45 to 60 seconds is the sweet spot on Reels.** In Socialinsider's 2026 data (about 9.8M Reels from brand accounts), 45–60 s Reels had the highest engagement rate of any length (0.35%), and Reels over 3 minutes dropped to 0.15%. Several sources say reach to non-followers falls off past about 3 minutes. Most of these numbers come from tool vendors, so take them as a direction, not a law.
- **Long stickman videos work when someone talks.** The silent metaphor hits (@thequietfixx, @stickmaxx) are 12 to 25 s. The long ones are voiced: @sticktalesanimations "Man will always be a man" runs 92 s with voice-over and big subtitles and got 3M likes and 25K comments. Silent works up to about 25 s. Past that, we need voice-over, dialogue or a narrator.
- **Series are numbered parts with a cliffhanger.** Stickman story series on Shorts and Reels run 60 to 100 s per part: a narrator in the third person, burned-in captions, and every part ending on a cliffhanger. One zombie survival series is reported at 4.2M views on a single part, but that number comes from a tool vendor's blog.
- **Romance is the core genre of "microdrama".** Vertical microdrama (minute-long episodes, each ending on a hook) is one of the fastest-growing story formats of 2026, and romance is its main genre. A dating app is a natural fit. Critics point out it buys "disposable attention, not long-term fandom", so a series needs characters people care about, not just twists.
- **Recurring characters turn viewers into followers.** The advice from people making animated series: write one sentence that fixes the character, the world and the recurring conflict, and check every episode against it.

### The rules we'll write by

1. **Hook in the first 2 seconds:** the conflict is visible in frame 1. No intro, no logo.
2. **A small payoff every 8 to 10 seconds:** a joke, a pop, a reveal. Long stories lose people in the flat middle.
3. **One twist at about 75% of the length,** then the payoff.
4. **The end either loops or leaves a question** ("Part 2?"). A series part always ends on a cliffhanger, and the caption says "Part 2 tomorrow".
5. **Voice or captions past 25 seconds.** Short lines, big subtitles, word by word.
6. **The app is the world, not an ad.** Rounds, balloons, questions and the queue are just how dating works in this world. The waitlist goes in the caption and the end card.

## The cast (same in every video)

- **Milo**, the quiet one. Small, thinks before he talks, writes long answers. The winner from Reel 1.
- **Ivy**, the popper from Reel 1. Calm, a bit dry, hates fake answers. Bun and dress.
- **Brick**, the muscle guy. Loud, flexes, means well underneath. The comic relief who gets a redemption arc.
- **Jade**, Ivy's best friend. Chaotic, brutally honest, pops people for bad grammar.
- **The Chorus**, the other three show-offs (sunglasses, car keys, "lol"). They show up as hecklers.

**The series sentence:** *In a world where everyone dates by popping balloons, Milo keeps winning by being honest, and everyone around him is still learning how.*

## Series: "Last Balloon" (5 parts, 45 to 60 s each)

Voice-over by a narrator (one dry, warm voice), plus characters' short lines as speech bubbles. Post one part a day, Monday to Friday, and pin Part 1 to the profile.

### Part 1: The quiet one (about 45 s)

Reel 1, extended with a narrator and a cliffhanger.

| Time | Picture | Narrator / text |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 0–3 s | Wide: four show-offs laughing at Milo, Ivy on the ledge in front of the moon. | "Every room has one guy nobody takes seriously." |
| 3–14 s | Answers come in, right to left: "lol", "my mom's lasagna", "reservations", "u". | "Four of them had answers ready." |
| 14–24 s | Milo's bubble grows while the others heckle him ("bro's writing a book"). | "He was still typing." |
| 24–28 s | His answer appears. Ivy smiles for half a second. | (silence, music drops) |
| 28–40 s | Pop, pop, pop. The pause. Pop on Brick. His balloon lifts him up to her. | "They laughed until he was the last balloon." |
| 40–45 s | Cliffhanger: they hold the string together. Below, Brick looks straight into the camera. Speech bubble: "this isn't over." | "Part 2." |

Caption: "They laughed until he was the last balloon 🎈 Part 2 tomorrow"

### Part 2: The first date (about 55 s)

He promised her fresh pasta. Now he has to make it.

- **0–3 s:** Milo in a tiny kitchen, apron on, flour everywhere, a phone propped up: "how to make pasta from scratch". Narrator: "He promised fresh pasta."
- **3–15 s:** Everything goes wrong, one beat at a time: the dough sticks to the ceiling, the lemon squirts in his eye, the parmesan grater shreds his thumb (a bandage appears). Each beat is a small payoff.
- **15–25 s:** The doorbell. He panics. Ivy is at the door, takes one look at the smoke behind him and laughs.
- **25–40 s:** They cook together. She's terrible too. The dessert catches fire. They look at each other, then at the fire.
- **40–48 s:** Callback: on the balcony eating burnt dessert, a speech bubble from his answer in Part 1 floats by: "dessert is whatever we burn together". She clinks her spoon against his.
- **48–55 s:** Cliffhanger: across the street, a window lights up: Brick, at his phone, in a new room queue: "FINDING YOUR LOBBY 5/6". The popper's profile picture is Jade. Narrator: "Meanwhile."

Caption: "He promised fresh pasta. He did not promise it would go well. Part 3 tomorrow"

### Part 3: Brick gets popped (about 60 s)

Brick's redemption. The funniest part: the comment bait is "be honest, are you a Brick".

- **0–3 s:** A counter labelled "ROOMS PLAYED: 1". Brick flexes. Instant pop in round 1.
- **3–25 s:** Montage, counter climbing 2, 5, 9, 14. Every time a new show-off move and an instant pop: a flex, a car on the profile, a shirtless photo, "u up". Each pop lands on a beat, with Jade popping without even looking up from her phone. Brick gets more confused each time.
- **25–35 s:** Rock bottom: he sits on the edge of the cliff with a limp string. Milo sits down next to him, says nothing, hands him a pen.
- **35–48 s:** Room 15. Question card: "WHAT ARE YOU AFRAID OF?" The others answer "nothing 😎", "spiders", "commitment lol". Brick types, deletes, types: "being boring without the gym". Jade looks up from her phone for the first time.
- **48–60 s:** Round 2, 3. He survives. Round 4: only Brick and one other are left. Jade's pin hovers. Cut to black. Narrator: "Part 4."

Caption: "Popped 14 times. Then he tried something new. Part 4 tomorrow"

### Part 4: Jade's side (about 50 s)

Told from the popper's side, made for women. The pressure of deciding.

- **0–5 s:** Close-up on Jade's eyes, the pin trembling between two balloons. Narrator: "Everyone thinks popping is the easy part."
- **5–20 s:** Inside her head, her past rooms flash by: the guy who said "you're pretty for a…", the one whose six photos were all of his car, the one who copied his answer from ChatGPT (his bubble glitches). Each memory is a pop.
- **20–35 s:** Back to now: Brick vs a smooth talker in sunglasses whose answers are perfect, too perfect. She looks at Brick's honest answer again.
- **35–42 s:** The twist: she pops the smooth talker. His balloon bursts, and inside it is a second, smaller balloon with a red flag on a toothpick. He was lying the whole time.
- **42–50 s:** Brick's balloon floats over to her. He doesn't flex. He just waves, awkwardly. She laughs. Label: "MATCH".

Caption: "Popping isn't the easy part. Girls, what's an instant pop for you? 👇"

### Part 5: Double date (about 45 s)

The finale and the clearest call to action.

- **0–8 s:** Milo & Ivy and Brick & Jade on the same cliff, two balloons tied together. The Chorus watches from the valley below, still holding their limp strings.
- **8–25 s:** A quick game between the couples: Ivy reads a question card, all four answer. Brick's answer is long now. Milo gives him a thumbs up.
- **25–35 s:** The camera pulls back. Next to their cliff there's a row of empty cliffs, each with an empty balloon floating over a ledge, waiting.
- **35–45 s:** Text: "Your turn." The empty balloons gently bob. End card: Popdate logo, "Waitlist in bio".

Caption: "From last balloon to double date 🎈 Your turn: waitlist in bio"

## Standalone longer stories (45 to 75 s)

For days without a series part. Each works on its own, and all use the same cast.

### A. "A pop hurts for 1 second. A ghost hurts for 3 weeks" (about 60 s, narrated)

- **Hook:** Milo looks at his phone, and a literal ghost floats out of it and sits next to him on the couch.
- **Middle:** Over weeks (a calendar flips), more ghosts collect, one per app match that stopped replying. They follow him around: to work, to the gym, into the shower curtain. Each new ghost is a small joke.
- **Twist (about 45 s):** In a Popdate room he gets popped in round 2. He stares at the empty string, then shrugs, and the ghosts behind him fade away. Being told no closed the loop.
- **Payoff:** He walks into the next room, lighter. Narrator: "At least here you know."
- **Comment bait:** "How many ghosts are following you? 👻"

### B. Red flags (about 50 s, silent with sound effects)

- **Hook:** Ivy pops in on a room, and a small red flag sprouts above a contestant's head the moment he answers.
- **Middle:** The question "how do you treat waiters?" Each answer spawns flags: "depends on the tip" (one flag), "I don't tip, it's a scam" (five flags, he's buried), "my ex was crazy" (the flags spell CRAZY).
- **Twist:** The quiet contestant answers, and a tiny green flag pops up. Everyone stares at it like an alien.
- **Payoff:** She pops all the flag guys in one throw, and the pin goes through three balloons like a kebab.
- **Comment bait:** "What's the answer that gets you 5 flags?"

### C. The wingman (about 70 s, voiced dialogue, made for the @sticktales style)

- **Hook:** Brick in a room with an earpiece. Milo's voice: "Okay. Do exactly what I say."
- **Middle:** Milo feeds him answers through the earpiece. They go great. Ivy, as the popper, is impressed. Brick starts adding his own stuff on top ("…and also I bench 140"). Milo: "Why would you SAY that."
- **Escalation:** The earpiece starts glitching. Brick mishears everything: "be yourself" turns into "flex your shelf", and he lifts a bookshelf.
- **Twist (about 55 s):** The earpiece dies. Silence. Brick panics, then gives his own honest answer, the same one from Part 3.
- **Payoff:** He survives the round. Milo, offscreen, quietly closes his laptop and smiles.
- **Comment bait:** "Tag the friend who'd be your earpiece"

### D. How we met (about 60 s, narrated, emotional, the one people send)

- **Hook:** An old stickman couple on a bench, grey hair drawn as white scribble. A grandchild asks: "Grandpa, how did you meet grandma?"
- **Middle:** He starts: "It was 2026. There were four other guys…" Flashback in the same silhouette style: Round 1, his long answer, the guys laughing.
- **Twist:** Grandma interrupts: "That's not how it happened. You answered 'lol' first and then deleted it." Grandpa: "…we don't talk about that."
- **Payoff:** Back on the bench, they hold the string of a faded red balloon together. Text: "Some stories start with a pop."
- **Comment bait:** "Send this to the person you'd want to tell this story with"

### E. 1826 vs 2016 vs 2026 (about 45 s, era gag, the 9M format)

- **1826:** A ball. A lady drops a handkerchief. A stickman picks it up and bows. Married next frame.
- **2016:** A stickman swipes until his thumb smokes. 0 matches, then 1 match that says "hey" and vanishes.
- **2026:** A Popdate room. Four rounds in 20 seconds, a match, a date, done. A clock shows "20 MIN".
- **Twist:** The 1826 lady time-travels into 2026, joins a room as popper, and pops everyone for not bowing.
- **Comment bait:** "Which era would you date in?"

## Making them

- **Voice:** A narrator and short character lines. Record our own voices (most authentic, free) or use a text-to-speech voice. Keep each line under 8 words, with big subtitles word by word.
- **With our code pipeline (`stickman/`):** The rig, balloons, bubbles, pops and camera already exist. New things needed per part:
  - **Part 2:** kitchen and door props.
  - **Part 3:** a counter label and a montage.
  - **Part 4:** a memory flash effect.
  - **All parts:** a subtitle track synced to the voice-over.
  - **Effort:** expect 1 to 2 days per part with Claude.
- **Faster route for the longer ones:** generate the scenes with an AI video tool from a character sheet of our cast, then cut in CapCut with the voice-over. Keep the pops, balloons and labels in our code style so it still looks like us.
- **Order:**
  - Post Part 1 first, because the scene already exists and only needs voice-over and the cliffhanger ending.
  - If it does better than the 22 s silent version, make the series.
  - If not, go back to short silent Reels and use the standalone stories as voiced experiments.
- **Measure:** completion rate and the number of "part 2?" comments. A series part that doesn't get "part 2" comments means the cliffhanger isn't working.
