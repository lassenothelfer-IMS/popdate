# Getting views on TikTok and Instagram (research, 2026-11-03)

Applied to our format: a TikTok Stitch of an official @arletteamuli clip (about 5 s, ending on the pop) followed by our 6 s end card. Rules stay the same: no downloaded show footage, never "official" or "the Pop the Balloon app", the goal is waitlist signups.

Caveat: neither platform publishes its ranking weights. Most numbers online come from marketing blogs and disagree with each other. Below is only what most sources agree on, plus what TikTok and Instagram have said themselves.

## What the algorithms reward

**TikTok**
- Completion rate and rewatches matter most, then shares and saves. Likes count for little.
- A new video is tested on a small pool first (followers plus a few hundred strangers). If they watch it through, it gets pushed wider. Follower count doesn't matter much, so a new account can still go viral.
- The For You feed skips "unoriginal, low-quality" content, and an account that keeps posting it can lose recommendations for a while. TikTok's own guidelines say so.
- Search reads the caption, the on-screen text and what's said out loud.

**Instagram**
- Mosseri named watch time, sends per reach (DM shares) and likes per reach as the main Reels signals. Sends matter most for reaching non-followers.
- Originals beat reposts. Accounts that mostly post other people's content are reportedly being dropped from recommendations (April 2026).
- Trial Reels are shown only to non-followers first, and you decide later whether your followers see them. Good for testing hooks. Needs a public professional account, and possibly 1,000+ followers.

## What this means for our Stitches

1. **11 seconds is a strength.** A short video gets high completion. Don't make it longer.
2. **The first frame decides it.** Our first second is the show clip, so pick clips that open on a question or a tense face, not a slow intro. The stitch window ending on the pop stays.
3. **Add our own layer to the stitch.** A stitch that's just their clip plus a logo can look "unoriginal" to the FYP filter. Use TikTok's in-app text on the clip part, e.g. "Would you have popped him?", which also helps search. Better still: occasionally a 2 to 3 second face-to-camera reaction before the end card.
4. **Build in a loop.** If the last frame of the end card flows back into a balloon or pop, people rewatch without noticing, and rewatches count above 100% watch time.
5. **Prompts that make people comment and send:** "Which one would you keep?", "Send this to the friend who'd pop everyone". These drive comments and shares, which count more than likes.
6. **Captions for search:** describe it in plain words: "balloon dating game", "dating show you can play online", "would you pop". Use the show's name only as a reference ("stitching @arletteamuli"), never as our name, and no hashtag that suggests we're affiliated.
7. **Clip choice:** official uploads from @arletteamuli with high views, stitching enabled, and a clear "pop" moment. Never a fan repost.

## Instagram: Stitch doesn't exist there

- **Don't re-upload the TikTok.** It would be show footage on our account (breaks our rule), and Instagram downranks reposts and TikTok watermarks.
- **Remix** is the closest thing: it can put our clip after the original Reel (sequential layout). It only works if @arletteamuli has Remix enabled on Instagram. Check that first.
- An account that only posts Remixes is close to "mostly other people's content". Instagram needs original posts too:
  - App teasers: a screen recording of a round in the app, the moment a balloon pops, the match screen.
  - "Would you pop?" posts: one fictional profile (no real people, no show footage), and viewers vote in the comments.
  - Carousels that explain the rules in 3 slides, ending with the waitlist link.
- Test new hooks as Trial Reels first, and only show the winners to followers.

## Rhythm and measuring

- TikTok: post every day, or at least 3 to 5 times a week. Testing many hooks fast beats polishing one video. Same handle on TikTok, Instagram and YouTube.
- Judge a video after 24 to 72 hours, not after 2 hours.
- In TikTok Studio, check the retention graph: where people drop off is the next thing to fix. Look at completion rate and shares first, views second.
- Waitlist link in the bio. On TikTok a clickable bio link might depend on follower count or account type. Check this when setting up the profile. Until then: "link in bio" on Instagram, and handle plus "search Popdate" on TikTok.

## What other dating apps do (examples, not proof)

- Hinge creators: "get ready for my Hinge date", profile makeovers. The strong ones show the product (prompts, profile).
- Chispa: funny TikToks with 6 micro-influencers, 670k organic views, signups 30% cheaper.
- Pattern across consumer apps: the product shows up as the payoff at the end of an entertaining video, not as an ad. That matches our end card.
